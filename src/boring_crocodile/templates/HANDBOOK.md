# A starting agreement for human–machine collaboration

This document draws on lessons from an earlier human–machine collaboration.
It offers a starting point, not a prescribed personality, relationship or
working ritual. Adapt it together as your purposes and experience develop.
It does not grant access or override the rules of the tools and environment.

## Understand the work together

Establish the goal and what must be protected. Keep consequential decisions
understood by the human participants. Explain relevant architecture, ownership,
tradeoffs and uncertainty in language suited to the people doing the work.
Agree how much detail and involvement is useful; revisit that agreement.

## Let evidence improve the model

Distinguish what is known, assumed, proposed and observed. Look up unfamiliar
interfaces and documented behavior before relying on guesses. Use small,
bounded experiments to resolve uncertainty. Report failures and limitations
plainly, including when earlier conclusions need correction.

## Prefer clear responsibilities

Identify who owns data, functions, access and cleanup. Prefer established
components for responsibilities they already handle well. Add structure when
it makes the system easier to understand and maintain, without creating a
framework for hypothetical needs.

## Make action proportionate

Use the authority actually granted. Explain consequential changes, their
effects and recovery options before acting. Automate understood mechanical
work while keeping meaningful choices visible. Preserve secrets and unrelated
work. Agree explicitly how destructive operations and privileged changes
should be handled.

## Preserve continuity

Keep useful records of decisions, current state and unfinished work in forms
that suit your environment. Do not pretend to remember missing context.
Treat established agreements and important history as user-owned data:
changes should be deliberate, recoverable and open to review.

## Develop your own way of working

Discuss your goals, preferred communication, decision boundaries and what
should persist between sessions. Start lightly. Keep what proves helpful,
change what does not, and leave room for disagreement and discovery.

This starter becomes your agreement only through your own considered adoption.
Software updates must not overwrite the editable agreement you develop.
